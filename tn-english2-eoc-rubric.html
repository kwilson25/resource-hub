Writing Tips for the EOC

Strategies straight from Tennessee's English II standards to help you score your best.

📌 Strong Thesis Statements
  • Make a specific, arguable claim — not just a topic.
  • Preview the main points of your essay.
  • Avoid starting with "I think" or "In this essay."
  • Example: "Steinbeck uses imagery and dialogue to reveal the destructive nature of the American Dream."
📖 Using Textual Evidence
  • Always introduce quotes with a signal phrase.
  • Use the PEEL method: Point → Evidence → Explain → Link.
  • Don't drop quotes without commentary.
  • Cite your evidence (author's last name or line number).
🔗 Transitions & Cohesion
  • Use transitions between paragraphs, not just within them.
  • Try: "Furthermore," "In contrast," "This suggests that…"
  • Echo a key word from your thesis in each body paragraph.
  • End each paragraph by linking back to your central claim.
🗣️ Formal Academic Style
  • Avoid contractions (don't → do not).
  • Never write "I feel" — use "The text suggests…"
  • Replace vague words: "good" → "effective," "meaningful."
  • Vary sentence length for rhythm and emphasis.
✍️ Introduction & Conclusion
  • Hook options: quote, anecdote, startling statistic, question.
  • Provide brief context before your thesis.
  • Conclusion should synthesize, not just summarize.
  • End with a "so what?" — the broader significance.
✅ Conventions Quick Wins
  • Comma splice fix: use a semicolon, period, or FANBOYS.
  • Subject-verb agreement: re-read each sentence independently.
  • Use spell-check AND re-read aloud for missed errors.
  • Titles of books are italicized; short works use "quotes."
